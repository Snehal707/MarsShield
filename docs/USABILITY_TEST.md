# MarsShield usability test script

**Status: not yet run.** No independent participants have completed this test. Until they do, MarsShield makes no claim of user validation, and any results must be reported with participant numbers and dates.

## Participants

- **Students:** 5 people who have not seen MarsShield, are aged 15 or over, and have had no specialised training in radiation or planetary science.
- **Domain expert:** 1 person working in space radiation, planetary geology or planetary data analysis, who has not contributed to MarsShield.

## Setup and rules

- **Session:** 20 to 30 minutes, one participant at a time.
- **Device:** start on the public site in a fresh private window, so the opening story shows. Run students on a desktop or laptop; if possible, run one student on a phone as well.
- **Facilitator:** reads each task aloud exactly as written below, and does not explain the interface or the science. When a participant asks a question, the facilitator replies: *"What would you try?"*
- **Think aloud:** ask participants to say what they are thinking as they work.
- **Recording:**
  - time to complete each task
  - whether they completed it unaided, with a hint, or not at all
  - quotes and observed confusion
  - screen recording, only with written consent
- **Data:** do not collect names in the results. Use participant codes S1 to S5 and E1.

## Student tasks

| # | Task (read aloud) | Success looks like | Observe |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Take a minute to look at the first screen. In your own words, what is this site about?" | Mentions Mars terrain, radiation, and that terrain can lower the dose | Do they notice the markers on the terrain? |
| 2 | "Follow the story until it shows what Curiosity measured. Before you get there, make a guess." | Sets the guess slider and reaches step 4 | Is the guess slider understood? Is it skipped? |
| 3 | "How much lower was the dose inside the notch, and how close was the model?" | About 19 % measured against about 10 % predicted; the model predicted about half the drop | Do they read the bars or the text? |
| 4 | "Was the model's prediction made without knowing the answer?" | No: the model's setting was partly derived from these measurements | Did they read or notice the caveat line? |
| 5 | "Plan a walk to the notch and back. Where should the astronaut stand while working, and why?" | Reaches *Where to stand*; says the model cannot reliably rank the spots, so the plan stays at the target | Do they understand "model cannot tell"? |
| 6 | "Pick the most sheltered spot instead. What changes, and is anything dangerous about it?" | Total dose changes; names the steep-slope and cliff warnings for that spot | Do the warnings update and get noticed? |
| 7 | "Does the astronaut use Curiosity's instruments at this site?" | No: Curiosity's objective is historical, and the astronaut's task is an assumption | Is the historical/assumed split clear? |
| 8 | "Open an experiment and find out whether the physical model beats the simple rule." | "Cannot tell them apart", plus a reason in their own words | Is the five-step sequence followed? |

**After the tasks, ask the student to rate each statement from 1 (disagree) to 5 (agree):**
- "I knew what to do next."
- "I could tell measured numbers from model numbers."
- "I trust the conclusions."

**Then ask, open-ended:** "What was confusing?"

## Expert tasks

| # | Task | Probe |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Go through the story and tell us whether anything is overstated." | Is the retrospective caveat sufficient? Is "about twice" fair? |
| 2 | "In Mission → Evidence, explain why the three notch figures differ." | Are the different references (±15-sol neighbours, the five-stop average, P6 against BG6) clear and correct? |
| 3 | "In the first experiment, judge whether the conclusion follows from the analysis." | Is the concentration at site 92 adequately conveyed? Is the site-level resampling appropriate? |
| 4 | "Is anything in Where to stand misleading for mission planning?" | Are the absorbed-dose and screening-rule caveats sufficient? Is anything missing? |
| 5 | "What one change would most improve scientific credibility?" | — |

## Reporting template

For each task, report:
- completion: unaided, with a hint, or not completed
- median time
- the main confusion, with a quote

Then:
- the expert's critique, verbatim where possible
- the changes made in response
- a list of tasks that failed for two or more students

Report results as observed, including failures. A sample of six people is a qualitative check, not a statistical validation.
